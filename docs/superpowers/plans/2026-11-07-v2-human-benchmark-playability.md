# Bringing v2 generation closer to the human playability benchmark

**Implementation plan · 2026-11-07 · Control version `2.0.1.app1`**

**Goal.** Reduce uncomfortable fast horizontal sequences, improve two-hand placement, and restore gradual, readable changes of cut axis. Judge progress on the final playable packages at comparable note density and by playing the passages that motivated the work.

**Approach.** Start with a direction-only change that has a known legal solution. Test slow-gap flow, position selection and hand assignment as separate experiments. Keep the seven-stage CPU inference pipeline, current model bundles, existing Beat Intensity speed scaling and package formats. M1–M5 stay offline measures. The generator uses local musical and geometric context and never needs the human corpus at runtime. Retraining is a separate decision if the available model proposals prove insufficient.

**Execution.** Work through the checkboxes on the development branch. Each generation experiment starts disabled, has its own paired comparison against the app control, and can be reverted independently. For agentic implementation use `superpowers:subagent-driven-development` or `superpowers:executing-plans`.

## Implementation record

The CPU generator and its paired evaluation tools are implemented on
`main_app_dev`. Across 120 app-control charts and 840 corrected development
candidates, all exports passed the package and protected-note checks. The
single reserved arm runs M1, M2, M3, M4 and late restoration of 90°/45°
turns at 500/650 ms. The isolated-recut experiment added little on this panel.
The dwell reranker and hand-recovery experiment remain available independently.

The selected arm reduced M1–M4 excesses on development. Its M5-share closure
was 8.4% against a 15% requirement, and 13 cut-axis dwell endpoints exceeded
their harm margins. No development arm qualified for promotion. A blind
playtest and one descriptive reserved comparison were chosen under the
[recorded selection protocol](../../../../Data/training/evaluate_beats/results/app1-playability-candidates-20261107/selection-protocol.md).
Production keeps the app1 defaults. All 240 reserved pairs passed package
and protected-note checks. Reserved M1 closure was 19.0%, M2 was 71.7% and
M4 was 57.1%. M3 fell below the matched human lower quartile in 233 charts.
M5-share closure was 8.2%, with 12 dwell checks outside their margins.

## What the control actually does

The archived current-version subset contains **278 songs and 1,112 Standard beatmaps**. The human reference is the highest Standard difficulty from **51 of 60** curated packages. The measured-NPS bands <3, 3–5, 5–7 and 7+ contain 7, 27, 7 and 10 human maps. These flags are an inventory of problems to investigate. A flag is a corpus-relative signal, and multiple flags can belong to the same map. [Scorer design](../specs/2026-11-06-playability-metrics-design.md) · [M5 report and individual package skips](../../../../Data/training/evaluate_beats/results/playability-m5-by-app-20261107/comparison.md)

| Signal | Flagged in app1 | What the experiments should improve |
|---|---:|---|
| M1 lateral load at speed | 970 / 1,112 (87.2%) | Fewer severe fast horizontal arrivals and uninterrupted horizontal reversals. Check the ≤200 ms and 200–350 ms bands and the longest same-hand runs. |
| M2 note occlusion | 426 (38.3%) | Reduce close, rapid low-to-high rises behind an earlier note, while retaining row and lane variety. |
| M3 bimanual divergence | 594 (53.4%) | Improve coordination of hand **positions** in active phrases. Both aligned and left/right-mirrored travel count as coordinated. |
| M4 cross-hand interference | 593 (53.3%) | Reduce overlapping swing/travel corridors and wrist crossovers while retaining playable mirrored doubles. |
| M5 direction variety | 95 (8.5%) | Recover occasional well-spaced small turns and reduce excess fast reversals. Track per-hand cut-axis dwell as well as both formal M5 scores. |

Three observations determine the implementation order.

1. **Break the Horizon, 1:40.58–1:43.27.** The archived R7 NPS-5 chart has 12 consecutive right-hand horizontal reversals at 221–232 ms. The horizontal arrows were already present before the primary speed cap. Early flow recuts established most of the pattern and the diagonal cap removed a remaining diagonal. Eleven arrivals admit a legal single-note diagonal substitution, none a single-note vertical substitution with its two horizontal neighbors fixed. A joint edit admits one diagonal bridge and eleven vertical cuts, with both boundary arrows fixed, zero clearance residuals and peak local movement cost 22.31 against the archival cap of 26.67. The archived fixture is the package's `Easy.dat` member, regardless of the NPS-5 request. On a **separate** app1 seed-3 CPU run the cap was 24, and 29 of 58 fast horizontal reversals had their final direction supplied by a speed-cap recut. Eight of those 29 had a legal nonhorizontal alternative at selection, 21 with final neighbors. Both early recuts and the final sequence matter. [Passage](../../../../Data/training/evaluate_beats/results/break-the-horizon-speed-cap-20261107/passage.csv) · [Trace and reproducibility limits](../../../../Data/training/evaluate_beats/results/break-the-horizon-speed-cap-20261107/trace.json)
2. **Arrow turn and cut axis describe different things.** A horizontal→opposite-horizontal cut turns 180° and stays on one axis. A 135° arrow turn already permits a 45° axis change under strict flow. In the full app1 corpus, 99.08% of arrow transitions are ≥135° versus 87.71% in humans. After a raw 45° / 90° turn, the median time until the next nonzero turn is 192 / 188 ms in app1 and 480 / 469 ms in humans. Rotating an entire horizontal stream to vertical can fix M1 while leaving both formal M5 scores identical. [Current-version turn breakdown](../../../../Data/training/evaluate_beats/results/playability-m5-by-app-20261107/by-version-and-density.csv)
3. **An exact five-note recipe would miss both tails.** In the seeded 30-song app1 sample, the typical run before a 45° axis change was one note on each hand versus two in humans. Yet 22% of generated runs before a 45° change lasted at least five notes versus 13% in humans. A 90° axis change accounted for 1.43% of sampled generated changes versus 18.65% in humans. Before a 90° change the generated medians were four notes left / three right versus one / one in humans. The pooled 90° *time* difference reversed in the 5–7 NPS band. Match distributions within density bands and keep the hands separate. [Per-hand comparison](../../../../Data/training/evaluate_beats/results/cut-axis-dwell-app1-random30-20261107/comparison.md)

## 4. Improve placement where the two hands interact (M2, M3, M4)

**Files.** `map_creation/v2_postprocessing.py`, `map_creation/v2_pipeline.py`, `tests/test_v2_generation_pipeline.py`, `tests/test_v2_generation_postprocessing.py`, `tests/test_playability_metrics.py`. Introduce separate experiment switches for sightline, path interference and bimanual alignment. Run on the surviving primary-pass stream, with access to its original per-ID cap lookup. Start with the smallest one-cell legal alternatives. Keep the original position available and retain it on a tie. Run position proposals before the optional final M1 direction repair and recheck all affected edges after either edit.

| Experiment | Candidate decision | Focused regression |
|---|---|---|
| M2 visibility | For a flagged rise, compare moving one participant by one grid cell. Recompute the nearest eligible earlier note within 400 ms, outside the stack window, within one lane, and the downstream notes for which that nearest neighbor could change. Prefer a lower obstruction at the same onset. | Hidden rise improves. A simultaneous pair, a well-separated high note, and a candidate that hides the following note exercise the boundaries. |
| M4 paths | Rank close opposite-hand candidates using the shared cells of their swing volumes, predecessor-exit-to-approach corridors and wrist crossover. Respect the 250 ms pair window, 400 ms travel reset and mirrored-double exception. Include each moved note's following same-hand corridor. | A legal standing-note layout can still have a travel-corridor clash. Reduce it while preserving a playable mirrored double. |
| M3 coordination | Only act in a segment with resolved axes for both hands and elevated divergence relative to its human NPS band. Start with windows of up to four consecutive notes and the original cell or a Manhattan-distance-one neighbor for each note. Evaluate the effect on the **whole affected segment**, including both hands' neighboring vectors. Favor aligned or vertically mirrored movement and preserve distinct positions. | Orthogonal travel improves. A mirrored diagonal wave is kept. An edit that eliminates an eligible axis or makes the hand stationary cannot win by reducing scored coverage. |
